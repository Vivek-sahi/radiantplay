## The discussion list — read this first
Twenty-three cases below have no agreed expected behaviour. They are the reason this document exists: a test plan cannot be written past them, because there is nothing to test against. Grouped by how much hangs on them.

**Decide before any join testing is meaningful:**

1. **G0 — is cardinality declared or derived?** Every path hardcodes `many_to_one`.
  
  **What I'd propose (answering "what is the expected behaviour then?"):** we **work it out from the data and show it as detected, with the evidence, editable.** On picking the two key columns, count how many distinct values each side has against its row count. `account_id` is unique in `dim_accounts` and repeats in `support_cases` → that is 1-to-many, and we say so: _"Detected 1 : Many —_ `account_id` _is unique in dim_accounts, repeats in support_cases."_ The pills stay, pre-set to what we found, and the user can override.
  
  Why this over asking: it is **cheap here** (the mock rows are already in memory — it is a distinct-count, not a warehouse query), and it demos the thing worth demoing. Every competitor makes the user know their own keys; a canvas that reads the data and tells you is the differentiating moment, and it is the same move as the fact/dimension guess in the caching modal, which already works this way. Asking the user to declare it and then never checking is the one option with no upside.
  
2. **G7 — M:M is not expressible, and that is product parity.** The current product's Create Join offers exactly three cardinalities (Many:1 · 1:Many · 1:1) — no many-to-many — and our panel matches it. So the decided dedup rule's "don't dedup on M:M, warn and suggest a bridge" branch has no way to fire. Decide: do we **detect** M:M from the keys and refuse or warn, or is a many-to-many join simply not modellable and the rule's branch is dead copy? - ok
  
3. **G8 — does the declared cardinality bind the merge?**
  
  **In plain terms (answering "what does that mean?"):** you pick **1 : 1** in the panel. That is you saying _"each account has exactly one support case."_ You press Apply, and the preview shows **five rows per account**, because the real data has five cases each. Nothing anywhere says _"you told us 1:1 and the data disagrees."_ The pills you set had no effect on what you are looking at.
  
  So the choice is what that control is _for_:
  
  - **(a) A check** — we compare it to the data and flag the mismatch. Useful, and it is free once G0 derives it.
    
  - **(b) An instruction** — 1:1 actually changes how rows combine (keep one match, drop the rest).
    
  - **(c) A label** — recorded and passed on to Spotter, no effect on the preview.
    
  
  ⚠️ **(b) is a trap.** It would let a wrong declaration silently delete rows, which is worse than the problem. **If G0 is derived, (a) is nearly free and (c) is what already happens.** My recommendation: derive it, show it, don't let it change the rows.

## G — Join, step 3: type × cardinality, and what the rows should do
This is the group you asked to enumerate, and it is where silent wrongness lives. Every row here is about **what the merged output should be**, which is the thing no current surface states.

**Setup for all G cases:** `dim_accounts` (48K, `account_id` unique) joined to `support_cases` (4.2M, `account_id` repeating). So the true cardinality is 1:M from the dimension's side.
### G0 — the precondition
❓ **Cardinality is hardcoded** `many_to_one` **on every live path.** Until it is derived from whether the key is unique on each side, and shown as _detected_ with its evidence, every case below tests a constant. Decide: derived-and-editable (the `POCV2_STATUS.md` recommendation), or user-declared, or removed from the UI.
### Join type — what survives
| #   | Join type | Expected rows | Expected nulls | Today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1  | **Inner** | Only keys present on both sides. 4.2M cases that all match → 4.2M rows | None | ✅ merge drops unmatched (`keepUnmatched = joinType !== 'inner'`) |
| G2  | **Left outer** | Every left row survives. Left rows with no match get nulls on the right | Right-side columns null | 🟡 kept, but **all three outer types behave identically** — the merge only branches on `inner` vs not |
| G3  | **Right outer** | Every right row survives, including right rows with no left match | Left-side columns null | ❌ **Not implemented.** Merge accumulates left-to-right and never re-scans the right table for unmatched rows. Right outer silently behaves as left outer |
| G4  | **Full outer** | Every row from both sides | Both directions | ❌ Same root cause as G3 |

⚠️ **Tier 1 — this one lies.** Not because a prototype owes us a correct SQL engine, but because the join panel is the surface under active design: if we pick a join type, look at the rows, and the rows are the same for three of four choices, we are reviewing our own decision against output that can't distinguish it. Right outer also _loses_ rows it promised to keep. The cheap fix is honest inertness — offer fewer join types in POC V2 — not a real outer-join implementation.

### The dedup rule, stated once
The decided rule, from `research/fk-column-deduplication.md` and restated in `POCV2_STATUS.md`: **drop the duplicate key only when the join guarantees the surviving column is never null.** Safe on inner, and on outers that keep the "one" side. **Coalesce** on outers that keep the many side. **Never** on M:M.

❓ **Raised 2026-08-13, needs the product reference before these cases can be written.** Our Metrics pane is a **second tab in the left dock** (`Data browser | Metrics`, `metricsPane` scope flag), designed on the reasoning that no surveyed platform runs two rails. - our two-tab split may be **re-inventing** something with an established shape, and
